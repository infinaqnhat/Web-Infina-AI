Closing gifts for real estate clients are small thank-yous given at or soon after a closing. Done well, they cost little, are remembered for years, and keep your name in front of the people most likely to send you their friends. This guide explains why closing gifts real estate agents give are worth the money, how much to spend and what the IRS allows you to deduct, ideas by client type, how to personalize a gift, and the rules to check.

Why Closing Gifts Real Estate Agents Give Are Worth It

A closing is the moment a client is happiest, and a gift tells them you noticed. It also supports the thing that drives most of your business. NAR’s 2025 Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers, as summarized by Virginia REALTORS’ key takeaways, found that 43% of buyers used an agent they found through a referral, 18% used an agent they had worked with before, and more than 9 out of 10 buyers would use their agent again. A gift is a small step in staying the agent they remember.

How Much to Spend on Closing Gifts Real Estate Clients Remember

Start with a per-client budget that fits your income, then choose the gift. The IRS limits what you can deduct, so it helps to know the rule. Under IRS Publication 463, you can deduct no more than $25 of the cost of business gifts you give directly or indirectly to each person during your tax year. Incidental costs such as gift wrapping, engraving, or shipping are generally not counted toward the limit when they do not add substantial value to the gift. The IRS also expects you to keep the cost, the date, and a description of the gift.

That does not mean you cannot spend more. It means anything above $25 per person per year is a business choice, not a deduction. Ask your tax adviser how this applies to you, because the details depend on your situation.

Closing Gifts Real Estate Clients Notice: Past Clients and Partners

The same thinking works beyond the closing table. A past client who sent you a referral deserves a thank-you of their own, such as a handwritten note and a small local treat, given after the fact and never promised in advance. A lender, attorney, or inspector who helped a deal go smoothly can receive a note or a modest seasonal card, as long as it is not tied to sending you business. Keep a short list of these people and a simple calendar, so the thank-yous go out on time rather than when you remember.

A rough yearly budget helps. Multiply your expected closings by the amount you plan to spend per client, add a small amount for referral thank-yous and anniversary cards, and compare the total with the commission those clients and their referrals could bring you. The aim is not to spend the most but to spend steadily on the people most likely to call you again.

Personalize Closing Gifts Real Estate Clients Get Without Spending More

A personal touch matters more than the price. A handwritten note that mentions something specific from the transaction does more than an expensive gift with a printed card. Add a photo of the home or the family at the keys, if they agree, or a line about what you enjoyed about working with them. Keep your own branding light, such as a small card with your name, because a gift covered in logos reads as advertising.

When to Give a Closing Gift

Give the gift at closing or within a few days, while the memory is fresh. Many agents add two later touches: a short note a week after move-in, and a card on the home anniversary. Put those dates in your CRM when the deal closes, as our guide to using your CRM database for marketing explains, so they happen without you remembering. Order in advance, because delays near a closing are common.

The Rules for Closing Gifts Real Estate Agents Should Know

Gifts to clients are ordinary and legal, but a few points apply. Keep the gift modest and given as thanks, not promised as a payment for referrals, because RESPA Section 8 in 12 CFR 1024.14 bars giving or accepting a thing of value for referring settlement-service business. A gift offered before the deal to win a client can raise questions under state law and brokerage policy, so ask your broker before you offer one. Check whether your brokerage sets limits on gift types or values, and keep records of the cost and the date for tax purposes.

Also follow the same advertising honesty rules as everywhere else. If you share a photo of a client with a gift on social media, get permission first, and avoid wording that describes who a home is right for, as 24 CFR 100.75 on discriminatory advertisements explains.

Closing Gifts Real Estate Agents Should Skip

  • Anything overly personal. Clothing, perfume, or alcohol can miss the mark or be unwelcome.
  • Generic items covered in your logo. They read as advertising.
  • Cash or large gift cards. They can look like a payment and raise rule questions.
  • Anything you will not remember to follow up on. A gift with no note is half a gesture.
  • A gift offered as an incentive to hire you. That is a different matter from a thank-you.

Track Closing Gifts Real Estate Clients Receive and the Results

Record each gift in your CRM with the date, cost, and a note on how the client responded. After a year, count how many gifted clients sent you a referral or hired you again, and compare it with your spend. That tells you whether the budget is right. Final Thoughts

Closing gifts real estate agents give do their job when they are thoughtful, modest, and followed by a note. Set a per-client budget, know the $25 deduction limit, match the gift to the person, personalize it, give it at closing, and schedule the later touches. Keep within the RESPA and brokerage rules, and track the results. A small gift that shows you paid attention is worth more than a large one that shows you bought in bulk.
