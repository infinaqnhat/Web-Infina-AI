A good flyer for real estate does one job: it makes a person take one next step, such as visiting an open house, calling about a listing, or asking what their home is worth. Agents use flyers at open houses, in flyer boxes, on neighborhood doors, and in mail. This guide covers the types of flyer for real estate, what to put on each, layout examples, how to make one, the rules to check, and how to hand it out so it gets read.

Why a Flyer for Real Estate Still Works

A flyer is cheap, fast to make, and easy to match to a specific property or neighborhood. It works best as part of a plan, not as a stand-alone tactic. At an open house it gives visitors something to take home. In a flyer box it answers a question at the curb. In a neighborhood it tells people you are active nearby. Types of Flyer for Real Estate and What Each Needs

Pick the type first, because each one has a different goal and a different action. Real estate flyers for agents usually fall into five groups.

Keep to one goal per flyer. A listing flyer that also advertises your buyer guide, your valuation offer, and your last five sales gives the reader five reasons to do nothing.

What to Put on a Real Estate Flyer

  1. A headline that names the point. For a listing, the address or the main feature. For an event, the date and place.
  2. One strong photo. Use the best exterior or interior image you have, at print quality.
  3. Three to five facts. Beds, baths, size, one or two standout features, and the price if you are using one.
  4. One call to action. A phone number, a short link, or a QR code, not all three at once.
  5. Your name, brokerage, and required details. Add the license or disclosure information your state or brokerage requires.
  6. Your headshot and logo. Use the same ones as on your card and profiles for a consistent look.

Leave white space around the elements. A flyer that is crowded is hard to read in the three seconds a person gives it, so cut anything that does not help the reader act.

Flyer for Real Estate Layout Examples

You do not need a new design every time. These layouts cover most needs, and you can reuse them by changing the photo and the text.

  • Photo top, facts below. A large photo across the upper half, then a short facts list and a contact line. It suits listings.
  • Left photo, right text. A tall photo on one side with the details on the other. It suits events and just-sold pieces.
  • Big headline, one image. A bold line such as “What is your home worth today?” with a single supporting image and a QR code. It suits seller flyers.
  • Four-photo grid. A main photo and three smaller ones for a property with strong interiors, with the key facts in a narrow band at the bottom.

Whichever you pick, match the look to your practical real estate branding guide choices for color and type, so a flyer, a card, and a sign read as one agent.

How to Make a Flyer for Real Estate in Five Steps

  1. Choose the type and write the single action you want.
  2. Pick the photo and write the headline and three to five facts.
  3. Use a template in a design tool, or ask your brokerage for its approved templates.
  4. Check every detail, including the phone number, the address, and the date, and scan any QR code on two phones.
  5. Print a few copies on the paper you plan to use, look at them in daylight, and then print the full run.

Many brokerages supply approved templates with required logos and disclosures. Ask before you hire a designer, because using the supplied version may be a requirement.

Rules to Check Before You Print

A flyer is housing advertising, so fair housing rules apply to every word. Describe the property and the service, not the kind of person who should live there, as 24 CFR 100.75 on discriminatory advertisements explains. Phrases such as “perfect for young families” can cause problems.

If you are a REALTOR®, NAR’s guidance on logo use with a firm name says the terms may be used next to a firm name but not as part of it. Your state and your brokerage may also require your brokerage name, your license number, or a disclosure on advertising, so check before you print. Use only facts you can support, and only share sale details you are allowed to share.

How to Distribute a Real Estate Flyer

  • At the open house. Put a stack by the door and hand one to every visitor.
  • In a flyer box at the sign. Keep it full and replace weathered copies.
  • By hand in the neighborhood. Deliver them in person or hang them where local rules allow.
  • By mail. If you want to reach every address on a route, see the USPS page on Every Door Direct Mail.

Do not put a flyer into someone else’s mailbox. The USPS rules say matter without postage cannot be placed in, attached to, or hung from a mail receptacle, and a USPS FAQ on attaching flyers to a mailbox answers no to leaving one there without mailing it. Local rules on door-to-door distribution vary, so check them too.

Make the Flyer Measurable

Add a way to tell which flyer worked, such as a short link or a QR code that opens a page made for that flyer. A page that matches the flyer’s offer converts better than your home page, as our guide to building an open house landing page explains. Then record the source of each new contact in your CRM, as the one-page marketing plan template suggests, so you can compare flyers with your other marketing.

Common Flyer for Real Estate Mistakes

  • Too many goals. The reader does nothing.
  • A weak photo. It is the first thing people see.
  • Small text and low contrast. It disappears in daylight.
  • A broken link or QR code. Test it before you print.
  • Missing required details. Check your brokerage and state rules.
  • No follow-up. A flyer starts the conversation, and your next message continues it.

Final Thoughts

A flyer for real estate works when it has one goal, one photo, a few useful facts, and one clear action. Choose the type, keep the design simple and consistent with your brand, check the housing and brokerage rules, test any link, and put copies where the right people will pick them up. Then track which ones produce calls, so the next batch is better than the last.
