A real estate agent referral can mean two things, and most agents need both. It can mean how you earn introductions from past clients and your network, or it can mean the referral fee one agent pays another for sending a client. This guide explains both: how to build a system that brings in referred clients, how a real estate agent referral fee works between agents, and the federal rule that limits who can be paid for what.

Why a Real Estate Agent Referral Is the Biggest Source of Clients

Referrals are how most clients find an agent. NAR’s 2025 Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers, as summarized by Virginia REALTORS’ key takeaways, found that 43% of buyers used an agent they found through a referral and 18% used an agent they had worked with before. On the sell side, 66% of sellers found their agent through a referral or used an agent they had worked with in the past. It also reports that more than 9 out of 10 buyers would use their agent again.

That makes the referral system the first marketing piece to build, before paid ads or a new website. Our complete real estate marketing guide for agents shows how it fits with the other channels, and the one-page marketing plan template gives it a place in your budget and calendar.

Build a Real Estate Agent Referral System in Five Steps

  1. List your sphere. Gather past clients, friends, family, neighbors, and local contacts in one place, with a note on how you know each person.
  2. Stay in touch on a schedule. A quarterly update, a yearly home anniversary note, and a seasonal card keep your name current without a pitch.
  3. Make a direct ask. Tell people what kind of client you want and ask them to introduce you. Most people are glad to help, but they rarely think of it unless you say it.
  4. Make it easy. Give them one sentence to forward, or offer to write the introduction yourself.
  5. Thank them and keep track. Reply to every introduction within the hour, tell the referrer what happened, and record the source in your CRM.

The weakest step for most agents is the third one. If you never ask, the system runs on luck. A short script helps, for example: “If you know anyone thinking about buying or selling in the next year, I would be glad to help them. You can forward them my number, or I can write them a short note.”

What to Do When You Get a Real Estate Agent Referral

Treat a referral as a favor, not a lead. Contact the new person quickly, mention the person who referred them, and ask about their plans before pitching anything. Then tell the referrer how it went, without sharing private details. Our guide to using your CRM database for marketing explains how to track each source so referred clients get the right follow-up.

Real Estate Agent Referral Fee: How It Works Between Agents

A referral fee is a share of the commission that one agent pays another for sending a client who then closes. It is common when a client moves to a market where the first agent does not work. The usual process is to find a trusted agent in the other market, agree the terms in a written referral agreement between the two brokerages, and receive the fee at closing.

A written agreement should say who the parties are, which client it covers, the fee and how it is calculated, when it is paid, and how long it lasts. Fees vary by market and by brokerage, so there is no standard number to quote. Ylopo, a vendor, says its referral networks charge 25 to 40 percent of commission at closing in its breakdown of what real estate leads cost, which is one vendor’s figure, not a rule for agent-to-agent fees. Ask your broker what your brokerage allows and how payments are processed.

The Federal Rule on Paying for Referrals

RESPA Section 8, in 12 CFR 1024.14, says no person may give or accept a fee, kickback, or other thing of value under an agreement or understanding that settlement-service business on a federally related mortgage loan will be referred. The regulation lists exceptions, and one covers payments under cooperative brokerage and referral arrangements between real estate agents and brokers. The text limits that exception to arrangements where all parties act as real estate brokers, and it does not cover arrangements with mortgage brokers.

This is a summary of the regulation, not legal advice. State licensing law adds its own limits, especially on paying anyone who is not a licensed agent, so talk to your broker before you offer or accept anything of value for a referral.

Real Estate Agent Referral Gifts and Thank-You Notes

A handwritten note after an introduction is always safe and often the most memorable thing you can send. If you want to give a gift, keep it modest, do not promise it in exchange for a referral, and check your broker’s policy first. Following up with the referrer on what happened matters more than the gift, because it shows the introduction was worth making.

Referrals From Other Professionals

Lenders, attorneys, movers, and contractors meet people at the moment they are about to buy or sell. A relationship with a few of them can become a steady source of introductions, but the money rules above apply, so build it on trust and shared clients, not on payments. Show them how you work, refer to them when you can in good conscience, and make it easy for them to say your name. Your real estate agent bio and business card should be ready to hand over.

Track Your Real Estate Agent Referral Results

Record the source of every new client, and update it when a deal closes. After a quarter, count how many conversations and closings came from referrals, and who sent them. Thank your top referrers in person, and write to the people who have not heard from you in a year. The aim is a list of reliable sources and a calendar that makes sure each one hears from you.

Common Real Estate Agent Referral Mistakes

  • Never asking. The biggest source of business is also the one most agents never request.
  • Contacting people only when you need a listing. They remember agents who stay in touch.
  • Handing a referral off without follow-up. The referrer wants to know how it went.
  • Verbal fee agreements. Put it in writing, between brokerages.
  • Offering money to non-agents. Check the federal and state rules first.
  • Not recording the source. You cannot thank or reward what you do not track.

Final Thoughts

A real estate agent referral is the cheapest, most trusted source of clients, and it runs on a simple habit: stay in touch, ask directly, respond quickly, and thank people. When you refer a client to another agent, use a written agreement between brokerages and know the RESPA rule. When someone refers a client to you, treat it as a favor and report back. Do both consistently and your pipeline will depend less on ads and more on people who already trust you.
